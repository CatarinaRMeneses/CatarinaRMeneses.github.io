## Working Papers

"From Treatment to Safety: The Role of Substance Use Treatment in Preventing Intimate Partner Violence." [NBER Working Paper No. 34642, January 2026](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34642) (with Mevlude Akbulut-Yuksel, Dhaval M. Dave, Bilge Erten, and Pinar Keskin)
<details class="abstract-toggle">
<summary>Show abstract</summary>
<br><span class="paper-abstract"><em>Abstract:</em> Substance use is a well-established driver of intimate partner violence (IPV), with drug-related incidents posing persistent challenges for both public health and criminal justice systems. We examine how expanding access to substance use treatment (SUT) services affects IPV in the United States by leveraging variation in the opening and closing of treatment facilities at the county level. Using administrative data on IPV incidents from the National Incident-Based Reporting System at the agency level from 1998 to 2019 combined with county-level records on treatment facility information, we implement a continuous difference-in-differences research design. Our results show that adding three SUT facilities—the average annual increase per county over the sample period—reduces drug-involved IPV by about 1.5–1.7 percent. Staggered event-study analyses confirm parallel outcome trends, across treated and non-treated counties, prior to net facility openings and lend support to a causal interpretation of the estimates. Related evidence from SUT admissions drawn from the Treatment Episode Data Set (TEDS) shows that new centers significantly raise treatment entry, particularly among men, consistent with reduced perpetration driving the observed decline in IPV exposure. Our findings highlight the role of health services infrastructure in shaping violence-related outcomes and underscore the broader public safety benefits of investment in treatment access.</span>
</details>

"Clearance and Crime Prevention." (with Matthew B. Ross, CarlyWill Sloan, and Austin V. Smith)
<details class="abstract-toggle">
<summary>Show abstract</summary>
<br><span class="paper-abstract"><em>Abstract:</em> This project examines whether arresting offenders following violent crimes, specifically homicides and shots-fired incidents, reduces subsequent crime in surrounding communities. Using administrative data from the Chicago Police Department (2018–2022), the study employs a stacked difference-in-differences design that exploits variation in the timing of arrest clearance, comparing incidents cleared by arrest within seven days with similar incidents for which arrests occurred after 49 days. The analysis examines whether arrest affects subsequent community demand for police services, measured through emergency and non-emergency calls for service. Preliminary findings indicate no statistically significant differences in subsequent calls between communities experiencing quickly and more slowly cleared incidents. The findings contribute to the deterrence literature by examining whether arrest and the associated removal of offenders from the community generate measurable public safety benefits.</span>
</details>

## Published Reports

"Beyond the Numbers: A Qualitative Evaluation of the Connecticut Racial Profiling Prohibition Project for Addressing Disparities in Traffic Stops." [CTRP3, December 2025](https://www.ctrp3.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/CTRP3-Qualitative-Evaluation-Technical-Report-12.17.25.pdf) (with Ermus St. Louis, Amanda Nett, and Jillian Reeves)
<details class="abstract-toggle">
<summary>Show abstract</summary>
<br><span class="paper-abstract"><em>Abstract:</em> This project evaluates the implementation, effectiveness, and sustainability of the Connecticut Racial Profiling Prohibition Project (CTRP3), a longstanding statewide initiative aimed at reducing racial disparities in traffic enforcement. Drawing on 28 semi-structured interviews with Advisory Board members and police chiefs, the study examines stakeholders’ perceptions of the initiative’s successes, challenges, and opportunities for improvement. Findings highlight CTRP3’s role in strengthening data-driven accountability, fostering collaboration, and shifting enforcement practices away from discretionary and pretextual stops. At the same time, the evaluation identifies persistent barriers, including resource constraints, uneven departmental engagement, and limited community and frontline officer involvement. Building on these findings, the project offers recommendations to institutionalize reforms, strengthen community engagement, build departmental capacity, and ensure the long-term sustainability of efforts to promote equitable and accountable policing.</span>
</details>
